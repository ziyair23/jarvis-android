# L2 — Per-hour offer judging

## What he does
Drives with Jarvis running and the Dasher app open. A new offer card appears.

## What Jarvis does
Reads the card (already done by the accessibility service) and says one short line:
"{pay} for {miles}. {rate} a mile, about {N} an hour. {verdict}"
If the card has no time information, the "about N an hour" part is left out and the verdict uses per mile only (today's behaviour).

## How the time is found
- An explicit duration on the card, like "32 min" (3–120 minutes), wins.
- Otherwise a "Deliver by 7:42 PM" time, counted from now (wraps past midnight).
- Otherwise unknown.

## Verdict
Each measure gets a tier against his minimum: great (≥1.25×), ok (≥1×), borderline (≥0.85×), poor (below).
Minimums: per mile = his existing setting (default $2), per hour = new setting (default $20).
The overall verdict is the worse of the two tiers. Phrases: great "Good one, sir, take it." ok "Worth taking." borderline "Borderline, your call." poor "I'd pass."
He can change the hourly minimum by voice ("judge offers at 22 an hour") through the existing offer_rule tool.

## Acceptance tests
1. "$8.50 | 2.1 mi | 18 min | Accept" → pay 8.5, miles 2.1, minutes 18; ~$28/hr, $4.05/mi → great → "Good one, sir, take it."
2. "$6.00 | 1.5 mi | Deliver by 7:40 PM | Accept" at 7:00 PM → 40 minutes → $9/hr → poor even though $4/mile → "I'd pass."
3. "$7.00 | 3.5 mi | Accept" (no time) → per mile only → $2.00/mi → ok → "Worth taking." and no "an hour" in the line.
4. "Deliver by 12:20 AM" at 11:50 PM → 30 minutes (midnight wrap).
5. A "Deliver by" time earlier than now by hours (stale/odd) or a duration outside 3–120 min → treated as unknown, never a crazy hourly rate.
6. Card text with no "Accept" → no call-out (unchanged).
7. The spoken line stays under 20 words.
8. Nothing is tapped, accepted or declined by Jarvis; it only speaks.
