# L1 — Offer diary + best hours

## What he does
Dashes as normal. Later asks: "Jarvis, when are my best hours?" / "when do I get the best offers?" / "how have my offers been?"

## What Jarvis does
- Every offer the judge calls out is saved on the phone only: time, pay, miles, minutes (if known), tier. Duplicates of the same card are not saved twice (same 2-minute rule as the call-out). Keeps the latest 1500. Nothing leaves the phone.
- When asked, answers in one or two short spoken sentences from his own data, grouped by hour of day:
  the best 2-hour window by average dollars per mile (and per hour when known), how many offers that's based on, and the worst hour if there's a clear one.
- With fewer than 10 offers saved: says it needs a few more dashes before it can tell, and how many it has.
- He can wipe it: "clear my offer diary".

## Acceptance tests
1. Saving: an offer judged at 6:15 PM with $9 / 3 mi / 20 min is stored with hour 18, and the store keeps at most 1500 entries (oldest dropped).
2. Fewer than 10 offers → "I've only seen N offers so far; give me a few more dashes."
3. With offers in the 7–9 AM band averaging $2.80/mi and 5–7 PM averaging $1.60/mi (enough of each) → names 7 to 9 AM as best, mentions the average per mile, and the count.
4. A 2-hour window needs at least 4 offers to be named best (one lucky $20 offer doesn't win).
5. Reply is under 40 words, no lists, numbers written to be spoken.
6. Clear → diary empty, says "Offer diary cleared."
7. Available to Claude as a tool (offer_insights with action "summary" or "clear").
